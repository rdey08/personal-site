## Context

NMSU's computer science department was not short of student organizations on
paper. The problem was that they had gone quiet, and what activity did happen
ran on faculty energy rather than student energy: the programming competitions
were organized by faculty, even though they nominally sat under a student org.
What the department lacked was not a club but a student-run one with a reason
to exist beyond its own continuation, and something that pulled the whole major
together rather than one corner of it.

CSSA was founded in August 2025 to be that, and I served as its founding
president through May 2026, with **Dr. Bill Hamilton** as club advisor. It has
run continuously since.

## Building the organization

The calendar was the easy half. CSSA needed a reason for students to show up
more than once, so it was built around three recurring formats: programming
contests for practice, industry panels for the career question students
actually had, and social events for the part that keeps people coming back.

The harder half was who owned it. An organization that depends on faculty to
run its events is one bad semester from going quiet again, which is what had
already happened to its predecessors. So I recruited a full **officer team of
ten** and split the org into internal departments, putting each area in the
hands of a named student rather than whoever happened to have time that week.
That is the change I would point to: not the events themselves, but moving the
department's student organizing from faculty-run to student-run.

By the end of the year that meant about **thirty students at regular meetings**
and **roughly sixty at events**, across **five or six events**. The gap between
those two numbers is the honest shape of it: a large audience for the things
worth showing up to, and a much smaller core doing the work.

It also needed money that did not depend on any one officer. I secured the
club's operating budget: **Google** funds it at about **$5,000 a year**, with
the **FinTech Lab** and the **Hunt Center for Entrepreneurship** at NMSU's
**Arrowhead Center** also sponsoring.

## hackNMSU

The largest thing CSSA did was **hackNMSU, the first hackathon in NMSU's
history**, held on **April 10, 2026**. It drew **55 participants across 18
teams**.

I led sponsorship, judge recruitment, and event operations. Sponsorship was the
binding constraint: a first-time event at a university with no hackathon track
record has no prior turnout to point at, so every conversation started from
nothing. Judge recruitment had the same shape, needing people willing to give
their time to an event nobody had seen before.

The event was covered by the [NMSU
Newsroom](https://newsroom.nmsu.edu/news/nmsu-students-build--code-and-showcase-projects-at-inaugural-hack-nmsu/s/7bf856a4-f1e1-4831-a1ef-ced257bb6423)
and [Organ Mountain
News](https://www.organmountainnews.com/nmsu-hack-nmsu-student-projects-inaugural-event/).

## What this shows

The predecessors had not failed for lack of a constitution. They had gone quiet
because nothing about them was built to survive the students who started them.
Most of the year went into the parts that outlast a term: a ten-person officer
structure with real ownership, named sponsors instead of an ad hoc budget, and
a hackathon with enough of a record that the next one does not start from
nothing. The club still running is the part I would want judged.
